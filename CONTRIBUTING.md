# Contributing to ProjectOS

Thank you for helping make AI-assisted project work more reliable.

## Good contributions

- tested workflow cards;
- public case studies with measurable outcomes;
- clearer templates, quality gates, and handoffs;
- portability improvements across AI agents and work surfaces;
- corrections backed by primary evidence;
- documentation that reduces time to first successful workflow.

## Before opening a pull request

1. Describe the problem and the expected outcome.
2. Keep the core independent of a single model or proprietary interface.
3. Put product-specific behavior in an adapter or capability registry.
4. Preserve facts, sources, decisions, risks, and approval boundaries.
5. Add a verification method or reproducible example.
6. Check links, formatting, licenses, and secrets.
7. Update the changelog when behavior or methodology changes.

Do not submit credentials, private customer data, fabricated testimonials, or unsupported performance claims.

## Workflow proposals

A new workflow should include:

- a focused trigger and outcome;
- required and optional inputs;
- sources of truth;
- constraints and permission level;
- Definition of Done;
- automated, sampled, and human review steps;
- artifacts, handoff, and rollback;
- at least one successful pilot before it is marked stable.

By contributing, you agree that your contribution may be distributed under the repository's MIT License.
